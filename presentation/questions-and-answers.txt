AGENTCITY TWIN
Likely audience questions and suggested answers

1. What is AgentCity Twin?
It is a planning and decision-support workspace. An operator describes a city disruption, compares modeled interventions, and reviews the evidence behind a ranked recommendation.

2. Is this live Debrecen data?
The walkthrough uses a simulated disruption and modeled network inputs. The basemap represents real geography. A site can connect approved feeds with timestamps and supply measurements for calibration.

3. What produces the metrics?
A deterministic network simulation produces metrics and scores. The model gateway supplies advisory text to specialists using the aggregate evidence that the workspace allows.

4. How does the app choose a strategy?
Each candidate gets modeled outcomes at five timeline points. The workspace weights combine objectives such as traffic, safety, environmental impact, and accessibility. The decision view shows the ranking and the baseline for comparison.

5. What happens when specialists disagree?
If they favor a permitted alternative over the leading intervention, the orchestrator can propose a bounded revision. The worker simulates that option and records both scores. The revised strategy competes with the original candidates.

6. Who can execute a recommendation?
Authorized operators can record approval or rejection in the workspace. Operational teams execute any real-world intervention through their own authorized processes.

7. What happens if LM Studio is unavailable?
The gateway can try other eligible providers under workspace policy. The run retains rule-based findings when advisories fail. The trace and notifications record the failure.

8. Can sensitive data stay on site?
Yes. The Docker stack can reach a model served locally by LM Studio. Cloud inference requires policy consent. The gateway uses agent-level data permissions for what goes into a model request.

9. Which feeds can it accept?
Supported paths include GeoJSON, CSV, JSON, GTFS Schedule, GTFS-Realtime, REST, WebSocket, and MQTT. The organization approves endpoints and manages any required credentials.

10. How would you validate the estimates?
Collect observations with dates and comparable units. Fit the estimate on one portion of the dataset and check residuals against a held-out portion. Review the results with local operators before field use.

11. Can researchers reproduce the result?
The run records its seed, model version, input fingerprint, scoring weights, and exported evidence. With matching inputs and the same engine version, the deterministic simulation is reproducible. Advisory text can vary.

12. Can it support a different city or a larger workload?
An organization can configure another city with a connected network and demand pairs. PostgreSQL leases let several workers share a queue. Sizing and response times depend on the network, workloads, and provider capacity.

13. What would you need for a pilot?
One corridor and a named operator, the local road network and demand, observations with timestamps, a clear evaluation criterion, and consent for any external data or inference services.
